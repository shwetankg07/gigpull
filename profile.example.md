# Operator profile

Copy this to `profile.md` (gitignored) and rewrite it as yourself. gigpull
passes it to the **rerank stage only** — the deterministic scorer never sees it,
so ranking stays debuggable and stack-blind.

Describe your **situation and goals**, not your tech stack. The model is told to
judge whether an opportunity suits someone in your position, and explicitly told
not to favour work that merely resembles what you have already built. Listing
technologies here works against you: it narrows the funnel to what you already
know, which is the opposite of learning.

Second-year CS undergraduate, currently also CTO of a small profitable venture,
so I have real shipping experience rather than only coursework — I have taken a
product from nothing to live paying customers.

I am optimising for **learning breadth over money**. I would rather take work
that forces me into unfamiliar territory than work I already know how to do. Pay
matters but is secondary; an unpaid-but-interesting problem beats a well-paid
repetitive one.

Availability is flexible and I will work around the right opportunity. I have
university exams periodically, so short bursts of reduced availability happen,
but I do not want that used as a reason to filter opportunities out.

What makes an opportunity good for me:

- Someone will actually review my work and push back on it
- I own something end to end rather than being handed isolated tickets
- The problem involves systems — state, payments, scheduling, data — rather than
  purely presentational work
- Small enough team that I talk to whoever makes the decisions

What makes one bad:

- Purely repetitive delivery of something I have built many times
- No feedback loop, no code review, nobody senior to learn from
